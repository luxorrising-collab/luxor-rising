## Best-practice consensus (methodology)

**Readability** — aim Flesch Reading Ease ~60–70 (8th–9th grade). Most sentences
< 20 words; CTAs 8–15 words; paragraphs 2–3 sentences. No rigorous study ties the
score to conversions — write for the reader, use the score as a check, not a goal.

**SEO** — keyword in title + first paragraph + ≥1 H2; meta title ≤ ~60 chars;
meta description ~150–155 chars; descriptive internal links; keep it crawlable
(no key content hidden behind tabs/JS).

**GEO (getting quoted by AI answers)** — consensus across vendor guides (no
official OpenAI/Google guidance exists, so treat specific tactics as hypotheses):
lead with the answer in the opening; definition-first sentence per section;
extractable structure (short paragraphs, lists, tables, FAQ); cite sources, add
data and expert quotes; keep content fresh; strong traditional SEO still feeds
AI results because models use live search.

**Sales/copy** — short, clear CTAs; sell the structural gap, not adjectives;
trust (honesty, sources, named experts) is itself a conversion lever.

## Luxor facts (verify before use)

### Valley of the Kings
- General ticket ≈ **750 EGP** (~€14–16), admits **3 tombs** chosen from a
  rotating ~8–10 open at a time (sources also saw 600 EGP earlier in 2026 — check
  at the gate).
- **Seti I (KV17):** separate ticket, ~**2,000 EGP** (~€38–42); sources vary
  (1,000–2,000). The finest tomb; our pick over Tutankhamun every time.
- **Tutankhamun (KV62):** separate, ~700 EGP (~€15). Architecturally modest; the
  treasures are now at the Grand Egyptian Museum near Giza, not in the tomb.
- **Ramesses V & VI (KV9):** small separate ticket (~220 EGP seen).
- Rotation changes for conservation, often unannounced — any published "open
  list" dates fast. (This is the flagship article's core argument.)
- Opens ~6am; the 6:00–7:30 window is near-empty, by 9 it's coach crowds.

### Valley of the Queens / Nefertari
- **Nefertari (QV66)** is in the Valley of the **Queens**, not the Kings. Entry
  capped and expensive — a Nefertari supplement ~2,500 EGP (~€50) seen on top of
  a ~220 EGP Queens ticket. Confirm current cap and price.

### Medinet Habu (mortuary temple of Ramesses III, west bank)
- Ticket **EGP 230** foreign adult / EGP 110 student — official Ministry page, checked 2026-11-06 (aggregators still show 220).
- Hours **06:00–17:00 daily** per the Ministry page (no separate winter hours listed). Confirm the day before.
- Meaning (fact-checked 2026-11-06): small Amun temple sits on the Mound of Djeme (Theban creation mound, burial of the Ogdoad); Decade Festival attested from the New Kingdom, best documented Late/Graeco-Roman; earliest shrine by the Middle Kingdom (~2000 BC); migdol gate probably ceremonial; 18 m wall height is a reconstruction; 'second death' = final annihilation, not 'being forgotten'.
- Known for exceptionally well-preserved reliefs and surviving pigment; Syrian-
  style fortified gate; Sea Peoples battle reliefs. A 2026 restoration of a golden
  shrine (Ramesses III / Amun-Ra) was reported. ~20–30 min by car from central
  Luxor; allow 1.5–2 hrs. Best Oct–Apr, early for light and quiet.

### Grand Egyptian Museum (GEM)
- Near the Giza pyramids (not Luxor). The full Tutankhamun collection
  (~5,000 pieces) is shown together there. Relevant whenever Tutankhamun comes up.

### Nile sunset felucca (Luxor)
- Private sunset sails widely rated worth it. Wind-dependent — weak breeze means a
  motor. Afternoon timing lets you add Banana Island before sunset. Prices vary;
  ~$15 for a private hour seen; booking a boat directly is often cheaper than OTAs.

### Safety (Luxor / Egypt, 2026)
- Egypt broadly rated safe for tourists on main routes; US State Dept **Level 2**.
  **Do Not Travel:** Sinai and parts of the Western Desert near the Libya border.
- Luxor itself typically safe; normal petty-crime sense. West-bank sites fine by
  day with a driver/guide; avoid isolated desert-edge lanes/docks late at night.
- **Western Desert near Libya** is the real concern — remote desert only with a
  professionally licensed operator. Don't conflate normal west-bank archaeology
  with remote desert travel. Always re-check official US/UK/AU advisories.

### Valley of the Kings — re-verified 2026-11-07
- Standard ticket **EGP 750** foreign adult / EGP 375 student, 3 tombs from ~10 open (mid-2026: KV1, 2, 6, 8, 11, 14, 15, 16, 43, 47). Hours 06:00–17:00 (some sources 16:00 in winter). Card payment only; tram ~EGP 20.
- Extra tickets: Tutankhamun KV62 EGP 700; Seti I KV17 EGP 2,000 (no student discount); Ramesses V/VI KV9 EGP 220 (best value). Tomb of Ay WV23 EGP 200; Amenhotep III WV22 reopened Oct 2025.
- **Nefertari QV66 closed for conservation since March 2024**, no reopening announced (Aug 2026). Valley of the Queens is beside Medinet Habu.
- Tutankhamun treasure: Grand Egyptian Museum, opened Nov 2025, "more than 5,000 objects".

### Isis & Osiris — verified 2026-11-07
- Full narrative (chest, Byblos, 14 pieces) = Plutarch (c. AD 100) only; Egyptian sources allude (Pyramid Texts, Great Hymn on Louvre stela C 286). Isis name written with throne sign; "personification of the throne" is a disputed theory — soften. "Great of Magic" also used for other goddesses.
- Deir el-Shelwit: Roman temple of Isis ~4 km S of Medinet Habu; propylon Galba–Domitian, sanctuary Hadrian; ARCE conserved, opened Jan 2014. Love songs: Ramesside, some ostraca from Deir el-Medina.

## Karnak, Luxor Temple & Luxor history (verified 8 Nov 2026)
- **Karnak:** EGP 600 adult / 300 student, includes the Open-Air Museum (White Chapel); Mut precinct separate (EGP 200/100). Opens 6am; last entry ~4pm (official sources conflict on closing). Sound & Light still runs (~$20; times vary by season). Source: egymonuments.gov.eg/en/archaeological-sites/karnak; exploreluxor.org (2026).
- Great Hypostyle Hall: 134 columns, 16 rows, central 12 ~21 m, capitals 5.4 m, ~5,000 m²; Seti I, finished by Ramesses II (NOT Amenhotep III). Guinness: largest hypostyle hall. NOT "largest religious building" (that's Angkor Wat).
- Main axis = winter-solstice sunrise (~116°), since Senusret I (ICOMOS–IAU thematic study). Hatshepsut obelisk ~30 m, tallest standing in Egypt; fallen twin's upper part re-erected April 2022 (no longer by the lake). "About 30 kings" is unsourced — say "dozens of kings".
- "As above, so below" = Emerald Tablet (Arabic, late 8th/early 9th c AD) / Kybalion (1908); "as within, so without" is modern. Genuine Egyptian anchor: Hatshepsut's obelisk calls Karnak "the horizon on earth" (Lichtheim AEL II — copyrighted, quote only a few words).
- **Luxor Temple:** EGP 500 / 250. Official hours 6am–8pm, last entry ~7pm (the 9–10pm figures are from resellers). Ipet-resyt = "Southern Sanctuary" (NOT "southern harem"). Opet: 11 days under Thutmose III, ~24 (perhaps 27) under Ramesses III. Royal-ka reading: Lanny Bell, JNES 1985. Visible sphinx avenue = Nectanebo I (380–363 BC), ~2.7 km, reopened 25 Nov 2021 (not the "Golden Parade", which was Cairo's mummy parade). Paris obelisk: gift of Muhammad Ali 1830, raised 25 Oct 1836. Abu al-Haggag died 1244; mosque door ~9 m above the floor. 1989 cachette: 26 statues, Luxor Museum. Moulid boats = "perhaps an echo" of Opet, not proven.
- **History:** Mentuhotep II reunification ~2040 BC (chronologies vary). Homer Iliad 9.381–384 "hundred gates" (Butler, PD). PD visitor quotes saved: Denon 1799, Champollion 1828 (FR), Nightingale 1849–50, Flaubert 1850 (FR), Duff Gordon 1864, Edwards 1877, Carter 1923 ("wonderful things"; journal: "Yes, it is wonderful"). Christie writing Death on the Nile at the Winter Palace = unproven. Mark Twain never went to Luxor. "A third of the world's monuments" = myth.
