## Best-practice consensus (methodology)

**Readability** — aim Flesch Reading Ease ~60–70 (8th–9th grade). Most sentences
< 20 words; CTAs 8–15 words; paragraphs 2–3 sentences. No rigorous study ties the
score to conversions — write for the reader, use the score as a check, not a goal.

**SEO** — keyword in title + first paragraph + ≥1 H2; meta title ≤ ~60 chars;
meta description ~150–155 chars; descriptive internal links; keep it crawlable
(no key content hidden behind tabs/JS).

**GEO (getting quoted by AI answers)** — consensus across vendor guides (no
official OpenAI/Google guidance exists, so treat specific tactics as hypotheses):
lead with the answer in the opening; definition-first sentence per section;
extractable structure (short paragraphs, lists, tables, FAQ); cite sources, add
data and expert quotes; keep content fresh; strong traditional SEO still feeds
AI results because models use live search.

**Sales/copy** — short, clear CTAs; sell the structural gap, not adjectives;
trust (honesty, sources, named experts) is itself a conversion lever.

## Luxor facts (verify before use)

### Valley of the Kings
- General ticket ≈ **750 EGP** (~€14–16), admits **3 tombs** chosen from a
  rotating ~8–10 open at a time (sources also saw 600 EGP earlier in 2026 — check
  at the gate).
- **Seti I (KV17):** separate ticket, ~**2,000 EGP** (~€38–42); sources vary
  (1,000–2,000). The finest tomb; our pick over Tutankhamun every time.
- **Tutankhamun (KV62):** separate, ~700 EGP (~€15). Architecturally modest; the
  treasures are now at the Grand Egyptian Museum near Giza, not in the tomb.
- **Ramesses V & VI (KV9):** small separate ticket (~220 EGP seen).
- Rotation changes for conservation, often unannounced — any published "open
  list" dates fast. (This is the flagship article's core argument.)
- Opens ~6am; the 6:00–7:30 window is near-empty, by 9 it's coach crowds.

### Valley of the Queens / Nefertari
- **Nefertari (QV66)** is in the Valley of the **Queens**, not the Kings. Entry
  capped and expensive — a Nefertari supplement ~2,500 EGP (~€50) seen on top of
  a ~220 EGP Queens ticket. Confirm current cap and price.

### Medinet Habu (mortuary temple of Ramesses III, west bank)
- Ticket ~**220 EGP** (~110 EGP student) as of 2026; older listings show 100 EGP.
- Hours ~**6:00–17:00** (winter may close 16:00). Confirm the day before.
- Known for exceptionally well-preserved reliefs and surviving pigment; Syrian-
  style fortified gate; Sea Peoples battle reliefs. A 2026 restoration of a golden
  shrine (Ramesses III / Amun-Ra) was reported. ~20–30 min by car from central
  Luxor; allow 1.5–2 hrs. Best Oct–Apr, early for light and quiet.

### Grand Egyptian Museum (GEM)
- Near the Giza pyramids (not Luxor). The full Tutankhamun collection
  (~5,000 pieces) is shown together there. Relevant whenever Tutankhamun comes up.

### Nile sunset felucca (Luxor)
- Private sunset sails widely rated worth it. Wind-dependent — weak breeze means a
  motor. Afternoon timing lets you add Banana Island before sunset. Prices vary;
  ~$15 for a private hour seen; booking a boat directly is often cheaper than OTAs.

### Safety (Luxor / Egypt, 2026)
- Egypt broadly rated safe for tourists on main routes; US State Dept **Level 2**.
  **Do Not Travel:** Sinai and parts of the Western Desert near the Libya border.
- Luxor itself typically safe; normal petty-crime sense. West-bank sites fine by
  day with a driver/guide; avoid isolated desert-edge lanes/docks late at night.
- **Western Desert near Libya** is the real concern — remote desert only with a
  professionally licensed operator. Don't conflate normal west-bank archaeology
  with remote desert travel. Always re-check official US/UK/AU advisories.
